What we tested

We keep the definitions of Value, Profitability and Momentum fixed. Each factor buys the top 30% and shorts the bottom 30% of stocks with enough data. We then test whether model forecasts can help us change the weight of each factor.

Equal factor weights

Each factor gets one-third of the allocation. This is our main comparison.

Choose a model every six months

We use the last 18 completed monthly returns to choose a model. It still learns and forecasts each month.

Keep the starting choice

We also keep the first model or the first factor mix. These checks show what changing the choice adds.

This is a historical research study. We had already seen the evaluation period when we designed the six-month rule. The separate M6 result is kept in the comparison.

Data and method

Value is EBIT divided by enterprise value. Profitability is EBIT divided by average invested capital. Momentum uses the adjusted return from twelve months ago to one month ago, divided by annual volatility. We keep these definitions fixed for this experiment.

The 100-company list is our starting group. Companies need valid prices and all three scores to enter a monthly factor basket. Financial businesses stay outside the EBIT baskets. The notebook lists the companies and the monthly exclusions.

We keep time in order. The model learns only from completed earlier outcomes, then predicts the following month. Scaling and early stopping use earlier training data. LSTM, GRU and CNN can train for up to 100 epochs and stop early if their validation result does not improve.

We keep the original M6 model result. For the six-month rule, the model with the highest past mean net return above equal factors is chosen. The notebook contains the exact dates, settings, trading records and checks.
